# Process overview

## What I built

WEAVE is a two-room browser instrument. Song Studio turns six synthesised voices into moving coloured threads while players perform and rewrite three original songs; its authored events carry pitch, duration, velocity and chord quality. Piano Room offers a continuous 88-key instrument and a credited Beethoven performance transcribed as timed SATB voices. Both rooms synthesise every sound live, without recordings or downloaded samples.

## The moments that mattered

1. **Turning related loops into complete songs.** The first version crossed eight phrases but derived pitches from step numbers, so visual polish could not make it feel composed. I changed the harness first: every example needed a complete form, at least 32 lead events spanning seven pitches, explicit chord data and a six-voice live visual ([`1caf282`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-crit4-xty116/commit/1caf282)). I then wrote three lead themes with bass movement, harmonic changes and counterlines, added algorithmic reverb, made playback return to its opening, and rebuilt the interface around a ribbon stage and note-labelled arranger ([`c16c9ab`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-crit4-xty116/commit/c16c9ab)).

2. **Changing the interaction, then verifying the music.** Rather than stretch the grid metaphor, I used feedback to define ([`3df38da`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-crit4-xty116/commit/3df38da)) and build ([`0e881ce`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-crit4-xty116/commit/0e881ce)) a linked Piano Room. Electronic tone and a 37-key limit then triggered an 88-key rebuild, mobile navigator and Concert synthesis from detuned partials, generated hammer noise, stereo position and pitch-dependent decay ([`b33accd`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-crit4-xty116/commit/b33accd)). Its demo became a credited *Ode to Joy* arrangement ([`cf09328`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-crit4-xty116/commit/cf09328)), but listening feedback exposed flattened dotted rhythms and guessed accompaniment. I transcribed exact soprano, alto, tenor and bass timing from Mutopia's public-domain LilyPond source and protected the result with a complete-melody regression test ([`794b12d`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-crit4-xty116/commit/794b12d)). Forty-nine checks pass; browser inspection confirmed 88 keys and responsive containment.
