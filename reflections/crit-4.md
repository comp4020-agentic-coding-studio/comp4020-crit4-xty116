# Crit 4 reflection

## What was the breakthrough that moved the work forward?

The breakthrough was admitting that duration alone does not make a loop into a song. My previous version had eight named phrases, but the pitched voices were generated from the current bar and step. The structure existed in the interface more than it existed in the music. I replaced that shortcut with authored melody data: every lead note now has a MIDI pitch, duration and velocity; harmony events state their chord quality; bass and bell parts respond to the same progression. Giving each composition an Intro, Verse, Pre-Chorus, Chorus, Bridge and Outro then made repetition purposeful. The live ribbons became useful after that musical change because each line now visualises a genuinely different part of the arrangement.

## What did this work change about who I want to be as a software developer?

It made me more suspicious of satisfying the wording of a requirement without satisfying its experience. “Eight bars” and “six instruments” were easy facts to count, yet the feedback correctly noticed that the result still felt simple. I want to ask earlier what evidence a person will actually perceive. Here, model tests count melodic range and song sections, but the decisive check was letting the full 37-second piece play and watching it travel from its opening through the bridge and final resolution. Visual testing mattered in the same way: the mobile layout had no horizontal overflow, yet its now-playing strip still covered part of the set list. Fixing that required looking, not another width assertion. I want my future work to combine explicit contracts with that kind of patient listening and observation, especially when building software whose value is emotional rather than merely transactional.
