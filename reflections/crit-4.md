# Crit 4 reflection

## What was the breakthrough that moved the work forward?

The breakthrough was admitting that musical correctness, immediacy and authenticity were separate problems. I first replaced generated loops with authored melody data: every lead note gained MIDI pitch, duration and velocity; harmony stated chord quality; bass and bell followed the same progression. Song sections then became audible structure rather than labels. Feedback still found the grid abstract, so Piano Room changed the interaction instead of decorating it. A second response exposed another shortcut: 37 keys and electronic voices looked like a piano without offering its range or character. I rebuilt the keybed as all 88 standard notes and made Concert from live detuned string partials, generated hammer noise and pitch-dependent decay. Familiar keys, internal scrolling, pointer glissando and sustain now make the interaction direct while remaining within the no-sample constraint.

## What did this work change about who I want to be as a software developer?

It made me more suspicious of satisfying requirement wording without satisfying its experience. “Eight bars”, “six instruments”, “piano” and “responsive” are easy labels, yet a person can still perceive something as simplified. I want to ask earlier what evidence they will feel. Tests protect song form, synthesis wiring, A0/C8 endpoints and input, while browser inspection showed how one 2548px mobile keybed could remain playable inside a 390px page. The range exists structurally, but its navigator keeps each visible key finger-sized. I want future work to combine explicit contracts with patient listening and observation, especially when software aims for an emotional response rather than only task completion.
