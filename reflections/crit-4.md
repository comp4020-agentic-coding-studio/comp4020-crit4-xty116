# Crit 4 reflection

## What was the breakthrough that moved the work forward?

The breakthrough was admitting that musical correctness and musical immediacy were separate problems. I first replaced generated loops with authored melody data: every lead note gained a MIDI pitch, duration and velocity; harmony events stated their chord quality; bass and bell parts followed the same progression. Intro, Verse, Pre-Chorus, Chorus, Bridge and Outro then became audible structure rather than interface labels. However, feedback still found the grid abstract and unlike an instrument. Adding Piano Room changed the interaction instead of merely decorating it. Familiar white and black keys, pointer glissando, key attack and release, sustain and octave controls make the first action self-evident. The complete Glasshouse Morning event data now drives both the six-voice arranger and a live piano performance, so the new page remains part of one musical system.

## What did this work change about who I want to be as a software developer?

It made me more suspicious of satisfying requirement wording without satisfying its experience. “Eight bars”, “six instruments” and “responsive” are easy facts to count, yet a person can still perceive something as simple or indirect. I want to ask earlier what evidence they will feel. Tests now protect song form, melodic range, synthesis wiring and piano input, but looking at 1440×900 and 390×844 revealed different truths: desktop needed a substantial multi-octave keyboard, while mobile needed exactly one playable octave and a full-height composition. I want future work to combine explicit contracts with patient listening and observation, especially when software aims for an emotional response rather than only task completion.
